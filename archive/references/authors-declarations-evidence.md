# Authors and declarations: journal rules, standards and this skill's own rules

Read this when you need to explain a choice made in `parts/authors.md` or `parts/declarations.md`. Venues agree that authors are accountable people, that AI tools are not authors, and that data statements are expected. They differ in what must be disclosed about AI tools, in where each statement goes, and in whether funding is an acknowledgment or a statement.

Checked 2026-11-23. Verify each entry against the current page before citing it elsewhere. Policies on AI tools change quickly.

## What journals require

- **APS Journals Style Guide (February 2026).**
  - Names (II.B): authors in any order between title and abstract; at least a first name or initial and a last name; full first names recommended, in the same form across publications; authors with Chinese, Japanese or Korean names may add the name in its own script; ORCID identifiers are encouraged.
  - Byline footnotes (II.B.2): the contact author is given as "Contact author:" followed by the address; an optional footnote for equal contribution; "Present address:" for a later address; symbols in a fixed order.
  - Affiliations (II.C): only institutions where the research was performed; department, institution, city, state where relevant, country; either addresses grouped under the authors or numbered addresses linked by superscripts.
  - Acknowledgments (II.J): scientific guidance, technical help with the research and funding; not help with the manuscript, general encouragement, dedications or any mention of AI use; funding statements use author initials; placed after the main text and before appendixes and the statements.
  - Author contributions (II.K): a separate unheaded paragraph after the acknowledgments; the CRediT taxonomy is strongly encouraged, as initials followed by roles; a statement on the order of authors goes last in that paragraph.
  - Data availability (II.N): under the heading "Data Availability", after the acknowledgments and any contribution paragraph and before the conflicts of interest; supplied at submission; data references go in the reference list with creators, year, version, repository and a persistent identifier; a statement that no data were created is acceptable. Third-party software need not be cited in it.
  - Conflicts of interest (II.O): under their own heading below the data statement; they include funding sources, employment conditions and limits on sharing data, code or details; a fixed sentence is used when there is none.
  - AI use disclosure (II.P): substantive use must be disclosed, such as help with scientific reasoning or interpretation, drafting or revising scientific claims, literature synthesis beyond routine organization, and the generation of figures or the analysis of images; it appears under the heading "AI Use Disclosure" below the conflicts of interest; AI use is not mentioned in the acknowledgments.
  - Ethics statements (II.M) go in the methods text where the collection of data is described.
  - Order of the back matter: note added, acknowledgments, author contributions, disclaimers, data availability, conflicts of interest, AI use disclosure, appendixes, references. In Physical Review Letters appendixes follow the references as End Matter.
- **Physical Review Letters, Publishing Guidelines.** A data availability statement is required for all published articles. Substantive AI use is disclosed in the paper; authors remain responsible for accuracy; an AI tool cannot be an author. ORCID identifiers can be linked and are not added after first proofs.
- **Nature, formatting guide.** End notes after the methods: brief acknowledgements (no thanks to anonymous referees or editors, no effusive wording); a separate funding statement; author contributions by initials; a required declaration of competing interests; data availability and code availability as separate statements after the main text. The corresponding author handles enquiries and need not be the senior author. Equal contribution is marked in the byline; present addresses are marked.
- **Springer Nature, editorial policy on AI.** Large language models do not meet the authorship criteria. Their use is documented in the methods, or in a suitable alternative part where there is no methods section. AI-assisted copy editing of human-written text for readability, style, grammar and spelling need not be declared.
- **arXiv.** AI tools cannot be listed as authors; every author is listed in the metadata.

## Standards

- **ICMJE recommendations, "Defining the role of authors and contributors".** Four criteria, all of which an author should meet: a substantial contribution to conception or design, or to acquiring, analysing or interpreting data; drafting the work or reviewing it critically; final approval of the version to be published; agreement to be accountable for the work. Those who meet all four should be authors; contributors who do not are acknowledged. Written for medical journals and widely borrowed elsewhere; physics venues set their own criteria.
- **CRediT, ANSI/NISO Z39.104-2022.** Fourteen contributor roles: Conceptualization; Data curation; Formal analysis; Funding acquisition; Investigation; Methodology; Project administration; Resources; Software; Supervision; Validation; Visualization; Writing – original draft; Writing – review & editing. A degree of contribution (lead, equal, supporting) is optional.

## Where sources differ

- **What AI use must be disclosed.** APS lists kinds of substantive use; Springer Nature asks for the use of a large language model to be documented and exempts copy editing; other publishers ask for every use. `venue.md` records the rule in force; where it is unknown, this skill discloses.
- **Where the disclosure goes.** A section of its own (APS), the methods (Springer Nature), the acknowledgments or a form elsewhere. APS excludes it from the acknowledgments.
- **Funding.** In the acknowledgments (APS) or a separate statement (Nature).
- **Authorship criteria.** ICMJE requires all four of its criteria; many physics collaborations list everyone who took part in the experiment. This skill does not choose between them.

## This skill's own rules (no external source)

- That the agent decides nothing about authorship and declares nothing on its own; that the absence of competing interests is written only when the user has said so.
- The order of an acknowledgments paragraph (people, facilities, funding) and the rule that each thanks names what was done.
- The ranking of data statements from "deposited" to "on request". The studies behind the weakness of "on request" are in `references/supplement-evidence.md`.
- That a manuscript prepared with this skill is treated as having used an AI tool in a way that venues want disclosed, and that the skill declines to help omit a required disclosure.
