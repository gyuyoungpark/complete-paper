# Supplement: journal rules, studies and this skill's own rules

Read this when you need to explain a choice made in `parts/supplement.md`. Journals agree on one point: the main text must be complete without the supplement. They do not agree on how large a supplement may be, on whether it may contain text, on where methods and references go, or on how the main text must cite it. No study has measured a best size for a supplement or a best number of pointers to it; the two budgets of this skill rest on editorial precedent and on its author's judgment, and are marked as such below.

Checked 2026-11-23. Verify each entry against the current page before citing it elsewhere.

## What journals require

- **APS, Supplemental Material Instructions** (`journals.aps.org/authors/supplemental-materials-journals`, with a copy under each journal; the page did not load here and was read through search excerpts). Supplemental Material is useful information that is not essential for understanding the main result: multimedia, parameters used in or produced by calculations, derivations, further technical detail. It is not a way around a length limit, because "any article must be convincing without it". The files are not copyedited and authors see no proofs. A README.TXT with a description accompanies each deposit; file names appear on the published files; all files of an article form one deposit with one URL. All references cited in it are also listed in the reference section of the main text. As a rule it does not accompany Comments, Replies or Errata.
- **APS, how it is cited.** As an entry of the reference list, `See Supplemental Material at [URL will be inserted by publisher] for ...` followed by a brief description, and cited by its number in the text. Works cited only in the supplement follow that entry in the list, and the entry can say which reference numbers it includes.
- **APS, Publishing Guidelines of Physical Review B and Physical Review Letters** (read 2026-11-23). A paper must be understandable and convincing on its own. Editors may ask authors to move Supplemental Material into the main text or into appendices, or to turn a Letter into a longer article. Referees are asked whether the Supplemental Material is relevant and sound and whether any of it belongs in the main text.
- **Physical Review Letters.** The core of a Letter is about four journal pages (3750 words). Up to about two pages of End Matter may follow: appendices or other content that specialists will want or need; it does not count against the limit (announced 19 July 2024). A Joint Submission, a Letter with a longer article in another Physical Review journal, is offered as an alternative to a large supplement.
- **Physical Review Applied.** Appendices in the main manuscript are generally preferred over standalone Supplemental Material.
- **APS, data availability.** A data availability statement is mandatory (from 4 September 2024 in Physical Review D and PRX Energy, in the other journals by the end of 2024). Public sharing is encouraged, not required; authors who do not share say why and give referees access on request.
- **Nature, Extended Data** (Nature 498, 6 (2013); Nature figure guide). Up to ten display items that appear online and at the end of the PDF, are peer reviewed, and are cited in the text in the same way as the main figures. It was introduced because essential display items had been pushed into Supplementary Information.
- **Nature-family checklist** (Communications Materials, read 2026-11-23). Supplementary Information is one PDF, except for movies, audio and data files; it contains no essential display items and no Results; items are cited as (Supplementary Fig. 1), (Supplementary Table 1), (Supplementary Note 1), (Supplementary Data 1). Nature Communications asks for a source data file with one sheet per figure or table, supplementary ones included. Scientific Reports asks for one file whose first page carries the title and the authors.
- **Science.** Supplementary Materials consist of Materials and Methods, Supplementary Text, Figs. S1 and following, Tables S1 and following, and References. One reference list runs through the main text and the supplement; references cited only in the supplement do not count toward the limit. A manuscript in Science format seen here ends its main text with a list of the supplementary materials; that convention was not read on the journal's page.
- **NISO RP-15-2013**, recommended practices for online supplemental journal article materials. It separates Integral Content, essential for full understanding of the article, from Additional Content.

## Journals that capped or abolished supplements

- **Nature Cell Biology**, "Getting to grips with Supplementary Information", 14, 223 (March 2012). The number of supplementary figures must not exceed the number of figures allowed in the main text: eight for an Article, five for a Letter, two for a Brief Communication. Supplementary text with further results or discussion is not permitted. Supplementary data must be discussed in the main text, and where possible each supplementary figure, table or movie is tied to one main-text figure. Tables and movies have no set limit. Reasons given: a supplement that grows without structure is hard for readers to navigate and adds to the referees' load, and key data get buried.
- **Nature Immunology**, "On supplementary information", 13, 519 (June 2012). Supplementary figures should not exceed the number of main figures. Supplementary methods are no longer accepted; the methods section was enlarged instead.
- **Nature Neuroscience**, "Moderating supplementary data", 15, 339 (March 2012). A cap of ten supplementary figures for all article types; most articles then carried six to twelve. Supplementary information is limited to data directly relevant to the main conclusions.
- **Cell** (2009). One supplemental figure per main figure. Known here only through secondary sources.
- **Journal of Experimental Medicine**, Borowski, "Enough is enough" (2011). Essential supporting information only, no data dump, no additional replicates. Papers without a supplement fell from 57% (May 2005) to 16% (May 2008) to none (May 2011); items per paper rose from 2.4 to 4.6 to 5.9.
- **Journal of Neuroscience**, Maunsell, announcement of 11 August 2010. No supplemental material accepted from 1 November 2010. Reasons given: its size had grown until it approached that of the articles; reviewers put far less effort into it than into the article; authors and reviewers had entered an arms race of added material; an article should be a self-contained report. A cap was considered and rejected as arbitrary.

## What studies found

- **Who reads supplements.** Price, Schroter, Clarke and McAneney, BMJ Open 8, e021753 (2018): surveys at 17 biomedical journals, 2872 of 20340 answering (14%). 87% of authors had submitted supplementary material; 80% of readers said they had read some with the last article they read. For reading, further tables and figures were favoured over reporting checklists and raw data; for replication the preference was the reverse. A low response rate and one field.
- **Supplements that outgrow the paper.** Pop and Salzberg, BMC Bioinformatics 16, 237 (2015), an editorial with counted examples: a five-page article with 144 pages of supplement, a six-page article with 165. In one paper only 9 of 25 supplementary figures and 17 of 58 supplementary tables were mentioned in the main text. Supplements are reviewed superficially or not at all, and citations made only there are not credited. The authors ask what the right balance is and give no number.
- **Supplements disappear.** Evangelou, Trikalinos and Ioannidis, FASEB J. 19, 1943 (2005): supplementary files could not be reached for 4.7% of articles after two years and 9.6% after five.
- **Data held by authors disappear faster.** Vines et al., Curr. Biol. 24, 94 (2014): for 516 articles, the odds that the data set still existed fell by 17% per year.
- **"Available on request" rarely delivers.** Gabelica, Bojčić and Puljak, J. Clin. Epidemiol. 150, 33 (2022): of 1792 manuscripts whose statement promised data on request, 93% of authors did not respond or declined, and 6.8% sent data. Tedersoo et al., Sci. Data 8, 192 (2021), reach the same conclusion across fields.
- **What a deposit should look like.** Wilkinson et al., Sci. Data 3, 160018 (2016): the FAIR principles (findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable).
- **Links and reading.** DeStefano and LeFevre, Comput. Hum. Behav. 23, 1616 (2007), review 38 studies of hypertext and conclude that features such as links raise the load on the reader. Madrid, van Oostendorp and Puerta Melguizo (2009) found no difference in learning between three and eight links per page. These are studies of web text; none measured pointers from a paper to its supplement.

## What was measured here (2026-11-23)

- **The checker**: 52 cases pass (`evals/supplement/supp_cases.py`). Each copies the example manuscript, plants one fault or one layout (or none), and names the findings that must and must not appear.
- **Four manuscripts from arXiv**, the most recent listings in cond-mat.mes-hall for "spin-orbit torque" whose comment named a supplement and whose source held both parts in LaTeX. Four papers of one subfield are not a sample of the literature; they were used to find faults in the script and to see where the budgets fall against practice.

| | Layout | Main words | Supplement words | Display items, main : supplement | Supplement sections | Callouts | per 1000 words | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | full article; separate file that the main file inputs | 4772 | 1764 | 4 : 7 | 5 | 9 | 1.9 | 10, met |
| B | letter with theory supplement, one file | 2627 | 2280 | 4 : 0 | 3 | 7 | 2.7 | 6, exceeded |
| C | letter with theory supplement, one file | 2474 | 2376 | 3 : 0 | 6 | 7 | 2.8 | 6, exceeded |
| D | Science format, one file | 6626 | 98 | 5 : 15 | none | 22 | 3.3 | 14, exceeded |

  - Word ratios were 0.37, 0.87, 0.96 and 0.01: none exceeded 1. Display items exceeded the main text in A (1.75) and D (3.0).
  - B and C cite the supplement seven times each and never name a section. D has a supplement of fifteen figures and no text, and five paragraphs with two or three callouts. A has two such paragraphs.
  - In A, B and C, 4 of 5, 1 of 3 and 4 of 6 sections do not say in their first paragraph what part of the main text they support.
  - So the callout budget is stricter than the practice of three of these four papers, by one callout in two of them. That is intended: the budget states a preference of this skill, not a description of what is published.
- **What these runs changed in the script**: a main file that inputs its supplement; the list of supplementary materials at the end of a Science-format main text and the cover page of its supplement; finding the start of a supplement from the place where numbering is reset; contents lists no longer counted as citations; repeated findings merged into one line; a data statement written inside the acknowledgments.
- **Cross-file references**: the example compiles with `xr` in the order supplement, main, supplement, main with no undefined reference (pdfTeX, TeX Live). `eq_refs.py` resolves the prefixed labels.

## Where sources differ

- **How large.** Nature Cell Biology and Nature Immunology tie the number of supplementary figures to the main figures; Nature Neuroscience fixes ten; the Journal of Neuroscience abolished supplements and called caps arbitrary; APS sets no number and offers End Matter, appendices or a joint submission instead. No source gives a ratio for text.
- **Must every item be cited in the main text?** Nature Cell Biology requires supplementary data to be discussed in the main text. APS cites the supplement as one reference. A skill for another journal family (`advmat-supplementary` and its siblings) requires every item to be cited from the main text. `venue.md` decides; where every item must be cited, this skill puts the range into one callout.
- **May it contain text?** Nature Cell Biology: no further results or discussion. Nature-family journals have Supplementary Notes. APS names derivations and technical detail as typical content.
- **Where methods go.** Nature Immunology removed supplementary methods; Science places Materials and Methods in the supplement; APS journals use the main text, appendices or End Matter.
- **References.** APS: all in the main list. Science: one list through both. Other journals let the supplement carry its own list, which indexing services often do not read.
- **Is it reviewed and edited?** Extended Data is reviewed and styled. Supplementary Information and Supplemental Material are sent to referees and not edited; referees give them less attention (Journal of Neuroscience; Pop and Salzberg).

## This skill's own rules (no external source)

- **The ceiling of 1 to 1 for words as well as for display items.** Journals that set a number capped figures only, at or near the number of main figures. Extending the ceiling to words, and calling it a ceiling and not a target, is this skill's choice.
- **One callout per supplementary section; the budget of two per 1000 words; one per paragraph; none in the abstract or the conclusion.** The premise that each pointer out of the main text costs the reader is the judgment of this skill's author; the hypertext studies above are consistent with it and do not test it.
- **Reach in place of "cited in the main text".** An item must be cited somewhere, and every section must be reachable from a callout. A contents list or cover page does not count as a citation.
- **The four-way test** (main text, supplement, data package, cut) and the rule that whatever can be used without explanation is data. Sources ask for deposits and for small supplements; joining the two into one decision is this skill's.
- **The two-sentence opener** of each section, the order of sections by first callout, and the order of cuts when the supplement is too large.
- **Thresholds of `supp_check.py`**, all adjustable in the script: a table of more than 25 rows, a figure of nine or more images, a listing of more than 15 lines, three or more consecutive figures whose captions agree to 85% after numbers are masked, a contents list from six sections or 3000 words, an item cited more than twice in the main text, a callout counted as vague when it has 16 words or fewer and offers "details" or "more information".
- **One preamble for both files and `xr` for cross-references.** A convenience; a venue that compiles the sources itself may not support it.
