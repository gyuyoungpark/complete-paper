# Figures: journal rules, perception studies and this skill's own rules

Read this when you need to explain a choice made in `parts/figures.md`. Journals agree that a figure must be legible at its printed size and readable without colour; they do not agree on how large the lettering is, on the form of panel labels, or on images made by generative AI. The perception studies below were run mostly on simple charts read by general audiences, not on physics figures read by physicists.

Checked 2026-11-22. Verify each entry against the current page before citing it elsewhere.

## What journals require

- **APS (Journals Style Guide, February 2026, section on figures).** One-column figures are 8.5 cm (3 3/8 in.) wide; detailed figures may take one and a half or two columns. At journal size, lettering height and symbol width are at least 2 mm, data points at least 1 mm, line widths at least 0.18 mm (0.5 pt). Panels are labelled (a), (b) and cited as Fig. 1(a). Colours should have distinct greyscale values and be paired with different line styles; palettes should be accessible and nothing should rest on colour alone. Resolution 600 dpi or higher. Symbols and curves are defined in a legend in the figure or in the caption. Axis labels carry units in parentheses, for example R (10^3 ohm); tick values are integers where possible, written "0.2, not .2", with the same number of decimals along an axis. Lettering and notation match the text. Figures are numbered in the order of first citation. Use of AI in a figure is disclosed in its caption.
- **APS, "Style Basics" for Physical Review Letters.** The smallest capital letters and numerals should be at least 2 mm high when the figure is sized for the journal page. This fixes what "lettering height" means: the height of capitals, not the point size.
- **APS, Information for Contributors (Physical Review Applied).** Groups of figures that share a caption are labelled (a), (b); axes are labelled with correctly abbreviated units in parentheses; integer numbers on axis scales where possible.
- **Nature (final submission page; Nature research figure guide).** Standard widths 89 mm (one column) and 183 mm (two columns); 120 to 136 mm for one and a half; full page depth 247 mm. Panel labels 8 pt bold, upright, lowercase a, b, c; all other text between 5 and 7 pt, sans-serif (Helvetica or Arial). Text is not rasterized or converted to outlines; fonts are embedded as TrueType 2 or 42. Avoid coloured text. Arrange labels so that they overlap neither each other nor the data, and where practical avoid lettering over images and shaded areas. Lines between 0.25 and 1 pt. Keep white space small.
- **Nature Reviews artwork guide.** Colour is for grouping and for showing relationships; few colours; the most important element the most saturated; colours, shapes and orientation consistent across the figures of an article; only essential elements; one arrow style; the flow starts at the top left. (All but the first clause were read in an earlier session; re-check before quoting.)
- **Science.** Not verified. Two third-party summaries give different column widths (5.7, 12.1 and 18.4 cm; 8.5 and 17.5 cm). The official page did not load here, so `fig_style.py` ships no preset for it.

The two-column width of 178 mm used for APS in `fig_style.py` is the usual full text width (7 in.); the style guide, as read, does not state it.

## Policies on images made by generative AI

- **Springer Nature.** Images and videos made by generative AI are not permitted for publication. Exceptions: images obtained from agencies under contract that created them in a legally acceptable manner, and images directly referenced in a piece that is specifically about AI, reviewed case by case; exceptions are labelled as generated by AI. The policy names illustrations such as scientific diagrams among the image types it covers. Non-generative machine-learning tools used to manipulate, combine or enhance existing images are disclosed in the caption. The group's guidance page for researchers words the exceptions as legally sourced, directly about AI, or based on verifiable scientific data.
- **Elsevier.** AI tools may be used to create figures, images and artwork only in specific circumstances, which differ by category of image; for explanatory images the use is disclosed in the caption (tool, version, how it was used) and in the article's AI statement.
- **APS.** The policy page states that AI can be used for data visualizations, with the authors responsible for verifying the accuracy of all AI-generated figures or graphics, and that substantive uses are disclosed with the tool's name and version, how it assisted, and how the authors directed and verified the output. A third-party summary reports that the Journals Style Guide does not allow images generated or modified by generative AI. Read both APS documents before submitting.

Consequence for this skill: schematics are drawn by code or in a vector program, which every policy above allows, and the report flags AI assistance in writing figure code so that the declarations part can disclose it where the venue asks.

## What perception studies found

- **Ranking of visual encodings.** In controlled judgement tasks, position along a common scale was read most accurately, followed by position on non-aligned scales, length, angle and slope, area, and colour (Cleveland & McGill 1984, J. Am. Stat. Assoc. 79:531; Cleveland & McGill 1986, Int. J. Man-Mach. Stud. 25:491).
- **Review of the evidence** (Franconeri, Padilla, Shah, Zacks & Hullman 2021, Psychol. Sci. Public Interest 22:110). Position is the most precise channel and colour intensity among the least. Statistics of a whole display are extracted quickly; comparisons between particular values are slow, so the design should make the intended comparison the easy one. Labels placed on the data are read faster and with fewer errors than a separate legend, which taxes working memory. Rainbow and red-green colour scales create boundaries that are not in the data. Three-dimensional renderings distort values. A truncated axis can mislead, yet starting at zero is not always right. The evidence that removing "clutter" improves comprehension is mixed.
- **Colour maps.** Rainbow-like and red-green maps distort data through uneven gradients and cannot be read by people with colour-vision deficiency, about 8% of men and 0.5% of women; perceptually uniform maps avoid both problems (Crameri, Shephard & Heron 2020, Nat. Commun. 11:5444).
- **Colour-blind-safe palette.** Eight colours that remain distinguishable for the common forms of colour-vision deficiency (Okabe & Ito, Color Universal Design, 2002, revised 2008; Wong 2011, Nat. Methods 8:441). `fig_style.OKABE_ITO` holds these values.
- **Bars hide distributions.** For small samples, bar graphs of means conceal the data; show the points (Weissgerber et al. 2015, PLOS Biol. 13:e1002128).

## Guides

- Rougier, Droettboom & Bourne 2014 (PLOS Comput. Biol. 10:e1003833): identify the message; adapt the figure to its medium; do not trust the defaults; use colour effectively; avoid chartjunk; message before beauty.
- Rolandi, Cheng & Perez-Kriz 2011 (Adv. Mater. 23:4343): one key message per figure; start at the top left; build on a grid; sans-serif lettering; a limited palette; remove shadows, gradients and 3D effects; label directly.
- Technical drawing: in a parallel (axonometric) projection, lines that are parallel in the object stay parallel and lengths along each axis keep one scale, so dimensions can be compared on the drawing; a perspective projection looks more natural and gives both up.

## What was measured here (2026-11-22, matplotlib 3.11.2)

- **Capital height over font size**, measured from the rendered glyph "H": DejaVu Sans 0.729; Liberation Sans (metric-compatible with Arial) 0.688; STIX General 0.662; Liberation Serif 0.655. Arial and Helvetica were not installed; their published cap height is about 0.72. Capitals 2 mm high therefore need about 7.8 pt in DejaVu Sans, 8.2 pt in Liberation Sans and 8.6 pt in a Times-like font. `fig_lint.py --min-height-mm` measures the font in use and allows 5%.
- **The palette under simulated colour-vision deficiency** (Machado, Oliveira & Fernandes 2009, IEEE Trans. Vis. Comput. Graph. 15:1291, severity 1.0; distance in CIELAB). The closest pairs of the Okabe-Ito set stay at 17 or more (orange and yellow 17; vermillion and orange 18; green and purple 18). In matplotlib's default cycle, orange and green fall to 5 and green and red to 7. The lint's simulation agreed with the `colorspacious` implementation of the same model to within rounding.
- **Contrast against white** (WCAG formula): blue 5.2, vermillion 3.9, green 3.4, purple 3.1, orange 2.3, sky blue 2.3, yellow 1.3. Hence "yellow last".
- **matplotlib defaults**: `pdf.fonttype` is 3 (Type 3 fonts) and `svg.fonttype` is `path` (text as outlines); `fig_style.use()` sets 42 and `none`.
- **The lint**: 82 planted-fault and helper cases pass (`evals/figures/lint_cases.py`): each plants one fault, or none, and names the findings that must and must not appear. It has not been run on the figures of a complete published manuscript.

## Where sources differ

| Question | One position | Another | This skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lettering size | APS: capitals at least 2 mm (about 8 pt and up) | Nature: 5 to 7 pt | `venue.md` decides; no default |
| Panel labels | APS: (a), (b) | Nature: a, b bold | `venue.md` decides |
| Thinnest line | APS: 0.5 pt | Nature: 0.25 pt | `venue.md`; the lint defaults to 0.5 pt |
| Identifying curves | APS: a legend in the figure or in the caption | Franconeri et al.: labels on the data beat legends | labels on the data for up to about five curves, else a legend; never the caption alone |
| Coloured lettering | Nature: avoid coloured text | common advice: colour a label like its curve | black labels next to the curve |
| Removing ink | design tradition: erase everything that is not data | Franconeri et al.: evidence mixed | remove what the comparison does not need; no claim that less ink is better as such |
| Zero on the axis | always start at zero | Franconeri et al.: not always right | bars from zero; other axes free, with limits that hide nothing |
| Generated images | Springer Nature: not permitted, narrow exceptions | Elsevier: allowed for explanatory images with disclosure; APS: documents differ | never from an image model |
| Schematic projection | perspective looks natural | parallel keeps parallels and scale | parallel by default, weak perspective as an option |

## This skill's own rules (no external source)

- The premise that readers go to the figures first, and the three constants: one figure, one message; one colour, one meaning; one size, the final size.
- "One colour, one meaning" as a rule in both directions, enforced inside a figure and tracked across the paper in `figstyle.md`. Sources ask for consistent colour; the table and the lint are this skill's device. Black, white and grey are exempt as neutral. Two labels may share a colour when the table says so or when one panel shows them as points and line.
- Keeping the identification of series on the figure while moving explanation to the caption. The studies compared labels on the data with legends, not legends with captions; the extension is this skill's.
- No panel titles unless they tell panels apart; no figure title.
- Six hues at most; yellow last; one hue from light to dark for an ordered parameter.
- The numbers: up to about five labelled curves; six legend entries; three to six ticks; two or three text sizes; data lines about 1 pt and frames about 0.6 pt; six panels per figure.
- Schematics: the default view (azimuth -58 degrees, elevation 24 degrees), elevation between 20 and 35 degrees, three flat tones (top 32% towards white, one side at the base colour, the other 20% towards black), "flat first".
- The panel-level deletion and citation tests, and the order of precedence when rules conflict.
- Lint thresholds, all adjustable in `scripts/fig_lint.py`: two boxes overlap when they share more than 1 pt in both directions; panel labels are off the line beyond 0.5 pt and at different offsets beyond 1 pt; frames "nearly line up" between 0.35 and 6 pt; panels whose tops or left edges lie within 8 pt are treated as one row or column; two colours are the same below a CIELAB distance of 3; a pair is flagged for colour-vision deficiency when it differs by 20 or more for normal vision and by less than 12 in simulation, with no second cue; pairs under 10 are "followed only by order"; equal lightness means a difference under 8; a colour is neutral when its RGB channels differ by less than 0.12; a series is too pale under a contrast of 2 and lettering under 3; more than seven or fewer than two tick labels; more than six legend entries; more than twelve words of notes per panel; raster output under 300 dpi; default smallest text 6 pt.
