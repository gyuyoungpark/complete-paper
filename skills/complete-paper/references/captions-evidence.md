# Captions: journal rules, advice and reader studies

Read this when you need to explain a choice made in `parts/captions.md`. Journals agree that a caption must define what is on the figure; they do not agree on whether it should state the result. The reader studies below were run on computing papers and on charts for general audiences, not on physics papers.

Checked 2026-11-19. Verify each entry against the current page before citing it elsewhere.

## What journals require

- **APS (Journals Style Guide, February 2026).** Each caption must make the figure intelligible without reference to the text. Symbols and curves are defined in a legend in the figure or in the caption. Subfigures are labelled (a), (b), and the caption describes each. Lines should not be distinguished by colour only; add line styles or symbols and describe them in the caption. Axes carry units in parentheses. In the text, "Fig." is used except at the start of a sentence. Table captions are required, may be abbreviated sentences, and define the symbols and abbreviations that apply to the whole table.
- **Nature (formatting guide).** Each legend begins with a brief title for the whole figure and continues with a short description of each panel and the symbols used. If the paper has a Methods section, legends contain no details of methods. Legends are fewer than 300 words. All error bars and statistics are defined in the legend, as are unusual units and abbreviations. Scale bars replace magnification factors.
- **PLOS ONE (archived guideline).** A caption needs a label and a title; the title is concise and descriptive, 15 words or fewer; a legend is optional; credits go in the last sentence.
- **Science.** A cap of 200 words per caption is reported by a third-party summary; the official page did not load here. Treat as unverified.

None of these requires interpretation in the caption, and none forbids a title that states a finding.

## Two schools on stating the result

- **State it in the title.** Figure titles should communicate the conclusion of the analysis, with the legend explaining how it was done (Mensh & Kording 2017, PLOS Comput. Biol. 13:e1005619, Rule 7). AIP Publishing's guidance asks for a brief, declarative title for each figure. Editing guides add that a single sentence may state the primary result.
- **Describe only.** Other guidance holds that a legend describes what is in the figure and that results belong to the main text; PLOS ONE asks for a descriptive title.

The part takes the second position by default, on the author's instruction, and allows a declarative title where the venue's captions use one.

## What reader studies found

- **Many published captions are judged unhelpful.** In a sample of line-chart captions from arXiv papers in computational linguistics, 53.88% were rated unhelpful by graduate-student readers. Helpfulness correlated strongly with visual description and with stating a takeaway, and moderately with length (Huang et al. 2023, "Summaries as Captions", INLG; arXiv:2302.12324).
- **Four levels of content.** Descriptions of charts can be sorted into four levels: the chart's construction (marks, axes, encodings); statistics (extrema, correlations); perceptual trends and patterns; and domain-specific insight. In an evaluation with 30 blind and 90 sighted readers, the groups ranked the levels differently, and both judged the first level alone not very helpful (Lundgard & Satyanarayan 2021, IEEE VIS; arXiv:2110.04406).
- **Captions steer the takeaway.** When a caption emphasizes a feature that is also visually prominent, readers adopt it as the takeaway; when it describes a less prominent feature, readers follow the chart instead (Kim, Setlur & Agrawala 2021, CHI).

## How the part uses this

- In the four-level terms, the caption carries the first level in full, plus the facts needed to read the data (conditions, definitions of error bars and fits). Trends and insight are left to the main text.
- The studies warn that a caption limited to "what is plotted" can be unhelpful. The part answers in three ways without adding commentary: the title must name the quantity, the system and the comparison ("say what is compared"); every element is identified; and the hand-off test requires the takeaway to be stated in the text where the figure is first cited.
- This is a deliberate trade. If the author prefers the other school, the declarative title is the place for the takeaway, limited to one sentence that can be read off the figure.

## This skill's own rules (no external source)

- "Say what is compared, never how the comparison comes out."
- Defining a symbol in the caption even when the main text defines it. APS requires symbols to be defined in the figure or the caption; the wording here is the author's.
- The inventory step and the list of three things that are description, not interpretation.
- The word targets of about 30 to 80 words for one panel and about 150 for several.
